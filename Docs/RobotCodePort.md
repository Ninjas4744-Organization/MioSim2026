# Team code and MoSim behavior

Source: [Ninjas4744-Organization/Robot-Code-2026, commit 2bd34881f2c39f86a74cc7d3ea18ba6e00678493](https://github.com/Ninjas4744-Organization/Robot-Code-2026/tree/2bd34881f2c39f86a74cc7d3ea18ba6e00678493). The relevant Java files are retained in `TeamCode/`. This is a translation of command/state behavior into MoSim's robot framework, not a WPILib runtime.

The Look Hub heading-rate feedforward path is retained and uses MoSim tuning gain 1; the Java source tunable defaults to zero and its live dashboard value is unknown. MoSim derives bearing rate from chassis translation and filters it over 40 ms, independently of rotational launch-point lead. The simulator feed interlock additionally requires heading within 1.5 degrees and current wheel-speed readiness on every step, and pauses hub feed during sharp acceleration or rapid rotation.

Hub heading tolerance follows `12 − 1.9 × distance` degrees, with a 0.2 degree numerical floor and the source minimum distance of 2 m. Delivery uses 3 degrees. The firing state stays latched. A simulator feed interlock pauses the indexer while heading is outside tolerance, during a zone transition, or below the minimum hub distance. This extra interlock prevents firing a previously valid shot while rotating to a new target. Commented-out source transitions are not treated as active behavior. Manual Q shooting is a MoSim testing/driver override.

## Physical adapters

Motor rotations do not specify a rail length without the transmission geometry. The 0–11 rotation range is mapped to the existing CAD-based 0.240 m intake stroke. The hopper follows that measured extension over its 0.22946 m stroke. Force, speed, acceleration, and physical end stops remain finite. The source's `Box.java` is retained for reference, but `RobotContainer.java` does not construct a Box subsystem; its unused state machine is not presented as active robot behavior.

The source RPS commands are exposed separately from the simulated contact RPM. A hub contact-speed profile, interpolated through measured 2.25, 2.5, 3, 4, 4.5 and 5 m points, calibrates MoSim's official 0.65 kg fuel and release hood. Delivery instead solves a floor trajectory for the 58-degree hood and applies the source 58-to-55 motor-RPS sweep to that reference speed. Reusing the hub correction for a floor pass was incorrect. The rotor lookup table itself is unchanged. Longer moving virtual targets use a physical continuation anchored at the measured 5 m point, rather than saturating the contact-speed correction. A parameterized local traction adapter represents symmetric shooter tires and indexer grip: wheel RPM determines surface speed, fuel receives bounded forces while inside the mechanism, and the chassis receives the opposite reaction. Forces stop at release. No fuel transform or velocity is assigned, and no correction follows a ball in flight. This avoids speed spikes from competing point-contact impulses and balls spinning in place on a single inclined contact. There is no powered hopper conveyor in this model. A captured-fuel floor contact follows the measured retracting tray speed while feeding; it stops when the tray is not retracting. The front wall, moving floor, and net also move with the pump. The intake and indexer have shared force budgets of 600 N and 320 N across all contacts, so adding touching balls cannot multiply the motor force indefinitely. Incoming fuel receives separate transfer traction until fully above the tray, keeping storage contact cores out of the entry path. The funnel joins the narrow shooter guides at one continuous edge. These are contact approximations, not additional visible motors or alterations of the CAD.

The shooter waits for the modeled wheels to settle within 2% before and during feeding. That physical adapter is stricter than the real rotor controller's tolerance. Fuel remains an independent physical body: no storage parenting, launch teleportation, velocity assignment, homing projectile, or correction in flight is used.

MoSim does not supply TalonFX current measurements. The source's 82 A accelerator jam trigger is represented by occupied, stalled fuel flow, followed by the source's 0.5 s indexer reverse and resume sequence. The 75 A intake current trigger, real limit switches, encoder homing, vision measurements, and hardware faults are not faithfully measurable here. Physical rail position/stops replace the encoder/limit-switch inputs. The source's current-based intake save-outtake is not claimed as implemented hardware behavior.

Automatic mode changes also update a currently latched standard shot as the robot crosses zones. Auto Pass remains a delivery override. Delivery coordinates are mirrored from alliance-relative field coordinates into MoSim's centered field. The hub target comes from the actual MoSim hub node. MoSim's action rebinding remains available; the real PS5 button layout is not copied over the game's standard bindings. The normal simulation-speed release checks are authoritative; faster calibration runs are retained only as development evidence.

## Net

The installed 4414 robot was inspected in the game as a deployment reference. Its protected robot files were not decoded or copied. Turbo uses the public framework's `NetSolver` and `NetParentFollower`: rear lacing follows the fixed frame, front lacing follows the sliding hopper, and side lacing follows the telescoping span. Free nodes fold and collide with fuel. The roof transform keeps unit scale, and the former flat roof collision proxy is disabled. The original Blender model is preserved.

## Verification scope

Release reports record actual field-fuel collection and retention, physical rail bounds, empty and loaded carpet driving, cloth span, completed pump cycles, and actual hub-scoring events at 2.25, 3, 4, 4.5, and 5 m. Distance is measured from the robot center; each shooting case starts 30 degrees away from the required heading and uses the normal preload spawn flow. Firing positions are on the clear alliance side of the hub. Earlier neutral-side fixtures hit the hub's physical back panel and were excluded from release calibration. A separate obstruction fixture verifies the stalled-flow recovery command, its half-second reverse, and resumed scoring. The release tests use the packaged asset bundle and verify its DLL hash.

Additional v0.5 fixtures exercise both alliances, moving shots through normal keyboard actions, side deliveries from neutral, intake mesh displacement during hard pumping, and collecting thirty balls before loaded driving. See Verification.md for measured outcomes. The measured results cover those fixtures. They do not establish real-world shot accuracy, unlimited hopper capacity, every collision or field obstacle, or an exact match to hardware timing under all loads. WPILib, autonomous path execution, vision estimation, and climbing are outside this mod's implementation.

The supplied [qualification-match video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wm53yd1vaDw&t=191s) was reviewed at the match start and loaded/empty scoring cycles around 4:25–4:35. It is a visual reference for the intake, hopper and roof; the wide field view does not establish exact actuator travel or transmission ratios.

A packed hopper can resist full contraction with rigid MoSim fuel. The full-capacity regression retains the load through driving, allows 25 seconds for feeding with hard and soft pumping, then verifies empty stow. That window includes alignment and is not a measured real-robot throughput claim. It does not claim that thirty rigid balls can always fit the contracted hopper.
