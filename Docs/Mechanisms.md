# Mechanisms and calibration

The native model is metres/Z-up, intake +X, shooter -X. Unity metres are `(-nativeY, nativeZ, nativeX - 0.0223825)`. Exported axis markers verify scale and handedness. The bumper envelope is 0.8884 m wide by 0.79548 m long.

## Bumper contacts

Lateral contacts between the four bumper boxes and static objects on the Field layer now use a bounded foam approximation: 12,000 N/m stiffness, 120 N·s/m closing damping and an 8 mm hardening depth. The force budget is shared across each contact manifold. This prevents millimetre-scale opposing contacts from generating enough artificial friction to suspend the chassis above the wheels indefinitely. Hard contact resumes at the compression limit. Vertical support, fuel contacts, robot-to-robot contacts and the CAD bumper dimensions retain their existing behavior. These are simulator calibration values, not measured bumper material properties.

Lateral static-field contacts also use 0.03 static/dynamic friction on the robot's solid surfaces, including the hopper walls, shooter sides and moving intake. Native traces identified a hopper-wall contact outside the original bumper-only handler. A controlled pinch above the bumpers reproduced lost wheel support with both empty and loaded hoppers. Rigid panels retain their full normal collision response; only bumper foam receives compression. Upward-facing support and fuel contacts retain their authored friction.

The framework's root box is a trigger used for the bumper envelope. Four separate solid boxes now follow the CAD perimeter: 57.2 mm thick, from 32.28 to 159.28 mm above the chassis origin, with a hollow interior. Assembly and bundle checks require all four surfaces to be enabled, non-trigger chassis contacts that include fuel. The 0.6.0 build omitted these surfaces, allowing fuel through the bumper.

The chassis now uses discrete collision detection at MoSim's 4.5 ms physics step. Installed-player A/B tests with the same solid bumper geometry found that swept continuous detection held the chassis position while its reported velocity remained above 3 m/s. Discrete detection removed that clear-floor stop-and-jump behavior. [Native measurements](../Reports/native-dense-contact-audit.json) also retain the failing full-load scoring results; this contact correction does not establish that every complete cycle passes.

## Bellypan contacts

Three adjoining chassis boxes approximate the CAD bellypan at native Z 46.8–50.8 mm. The rear, middle and front sections preserve the large wheel cutouts while simplifying fastener holes and small edge details. They use the Robot layer and a low-friction passive sliding material, with solid fuel and field contacts throughout collection, reverse, stow and disable. The one-way intake keeper never opens their collision pairs. Balls beneath the plate can physically lift the robot. The latest private Windows grounded-clearance trial rolls loose fuel out of the low chassis footprint whenever at least one physical tire touches the field. It uses bounded horizontal forces and rolling torque toward checked floor exits, briefly releasing this robot's pinch contacts and restoring them afterward. Gravity, walls, other robots and other balls remain physical. It preserves the original balls and makes no ball-position assignments. With all four tires clear, fuel can still beach the robot. This gameplay assist is separate from the physical intake, stored-fuel and shooting transport described below. [Roll-out scope and evidence](../Reports/Rollout-20261124/README.md).

Assembly and bundle inspection validate the three sections. Integration diagnostics push stock fuel beneath each section and both joins in four mechanism states, then measure chassis support on stock balls and a field obstacle. Packaging requires all three bellypan checks.

The underside checks honor the loaded game's existing lower-chassis fuel compression allowance. Contact can compress a ball while its centre stays beneath the plate; no game-wide contact settings are changed.

[Development verification](../Reports/bellypan-verification.json) records the twenty underside probes, chassis support, six regression groups and full-field editor smoke replays. The latter retained 55 balls through the trench and 54 over the bump; they do not replace installed-player release certification.

## Swerve module contacts

Four solid chassis boxes cover the swerve housings inside the CAD corner cutouts. Each conservative envelope is 160 × 160 mm horizontally, between 50 and 150 mm above the chassis origin. The existing convex tire meshes supply the lower wheel profile and follow steering. Both housings and tires collide with fuel, including while collecting, reversing and disabled.

Fuel remains excluded from the suspension, procedural support cores and powered tread queries. Passive tire collisions can support or deflect the chassis, without treating rolling balls as carpet. The prior blanket fuel exclusion on every wheel child collider made the modules transparent and allowed balls into the bellypan cutouts.

The `Swerve` regression loads the packaged robot and sends stock dynamic fuel at each housing from two directions in four mechanism states, plus each tire at 0° and 90° steering. It requires actual collision callbacks, a solid collision pair, and no crossing through the module. Other robot colliders are isolated during each probe so the bellypan cannot mask a missing module contact. A final motor-driven clear-floor run checks ordinary driving and chassis height.

Trench geometry checks isolate loose field fuel while retaining each loaded case's own payload. `-turboFieldFuel` keeps the loose balls for separate obstruction diagnostics. With solid tire contacts, a ball beneath a wheel can physically lift the chassis against the trench crossbar; the failed loose-fuel trace is preserved separately.

## Bounded slides

The moving-bin correction changes the stowed storage envelope. Earlier fixed-bin full-load stow results do not certify this assembly; dense-load stow must be revalidated separately.

Automatic pumping follows fuel occupancy during hub shooting and delivery. It starts with a shot request, including aiming and spin-up, while balls remain in storage; a sparse load could otherwise leave before any pumping began. Storage detection excludes ground fuel beneath the chassis and balls already handed to the shooter. The calibrated 9–10 rotation soft stroke continues through jam recovery without restarting its phase. Automatic pumping stops when storage clears, shooting is cancelled, reverse is requested or the robot is disabled, and resumes if more fuel arrives while firing.

An explicit hard-pump request retains priority. The rail may first need to open to 10.5 encoder rotations before starting its three hard cycles; the automatic soft command cannot interrupt that opening or an active hard cycle. The recovery regression requests hard pumping after a recovery and requires all three cycles to finish.

[Sliding-hopper development checks](../Reports/sliding-hopper-r3/verification.json) cover three-ball hub shots, four three-ball delivery cases, an empty hopper, four physical travel/stow headings, eight angled field-trench cases and manual hard-pump priority. They measure the bin wall itself while fuel remains, in addition to the intake rail, and require the overlapping floor to stay inside the moving front panel. Earlier automatic-pump reports measured the intake-only assembly and remain historical evidence.

The hopper bin itself slides, matching the supplied pumping footage. Its CAD model, side walls, front panel, inlet lip, kicker and front roof anchors move on `hopperRail`; the rear indexer and rear roof anchors stay on the chassis. The overlapping tray bridges the moving front panel to the stationary indexer entrance, without leaving an invisible floor beyond the retracted bin. Each slide's configurable joint connects directly to the chassis. X/Y translation and all angular motion are locked. Z translation is **Limited**, with a symmetric limit of half the stroke and the connected anchor located halfway between closed and open. This defines a physical range of 0…0.240 m for the intake and 0…0.22946 m for the hopper carrier.

The runtime reads each moving body's position in the chassis rigidbody frame, rather than relying on a versioned encoder offset. Velocity commands use a braking-aware speed profile, limited to 0.16 m/s, 1.8 m/s², and 350 N. The lower pump speed and braking profile reduce reversal overshoot within the short 8–11 / 9–10 rotation windows. Pump velocity brakes toward the nominal source encoder windows before reversing. The simulator uses a 0.15-rotation upper reversal tolerance, matching the source controller position-goal tolerance, and a 2 mm upper braking margin; the hardware percent-output loop has its own encoder and virtual-limit timing. The same finite force cap and physical stops remain active when disabled; the disable path commands zero velocity without re-homing the joint or restoring infinite force.

The joint's 5 mm contact tolerance exceeds the controller's 1.5 mm settling band, keeping the limit active at the settled open position. This follows Unity's guidance that larger contact tolerances help enforce limits at high speed. The impact regression retains the original strict travel bounds. [Unity Configurable Joint reference](https://docs.unity3d.com/2023.2/Documentation/Manual/class-ConfigurableJoint.html).

Turbo configures solver settings for its three robot bodies directly. The automatically attached JointStackStabilizer is disabled on this robot because it caches child fuel bodies before the spawn handler detaches them. Detached or destroyed fuel must not remain part of the robot's physics-helper cache. Other robots and the game framework are not modified.

The game model's fully deployed pose is the source pose. Closing translates each selected moving assembly toward the chassis. These stroke lengths are simulation estimates based on the CAD envelope, not a conversion of the team's motor-rotation limits.

The shooting/pumping footage supplied by the user shows a compact front roller assembly directly beneath the hopper. The earlier 13-degree downward adjustment in source asset 1.3.1 was inferred from photos; undoing that adjustment restores the original CAD alignment. The current game model reconstructs 59 original CAD parts and removes the invented supports, shaped clearance bump and arbitrary mesh cuts from earlier visual attempts. `Tools/Blender/restore_cad_kicker.py` records this reconstruction; `Reports/hopper-kicker-model.json` lists every part and its assembly.

The kicker's original structural plates, motor housings and upper pulleys move with its front carrier. Original lower bearing plates, bearings and shaft (parts 51, 54, 56, 64, 68, 78, 94 and 95) form the lower hinged carrier; the original roller (part 82) remains separately driven. The deployed assembly retains its original CAD geometry, including its actual holes, fasteners and motor mounts.

The lower carrier stays deployed during normal hard and soft pump strokes, matching the footage. Full stow still raises it before either slide retracts. Initial opening moves the intake/kicker carrier out before lowering the kicker. A 60-degree visual retraction about native `(0.57204, 0, 0.24405)` m clears the bumper; this is clearance calibration, since the footage does not show a complete opening or closing. Fuel contacts and rail limits retain their calibration. The integration check verifies that the kicker remains down while pumping, and the mesh audit covers both the complete stow sequence and the pump window.

`Reports/Turbo4744-kicker-reference-comparison.png` compares the compact CAD assembly against the supplied footage. The older `Reports/Turbo4744-kicker-pumping.gif` shows an earlier assembly; the sliding-bin correction is checked separately against the newer supplied footage.

## Fuel and shooting

Eight instances of the official fuel prefab are stored in `RebuiltRobotBase.preloads`. MoSim's `RebuiltSpawnInitializer` detaches them into the game-piece world and removes the matching number of field pieces. Empty markers are not valid preloads. Fuel layers and colliders are preserved.

The intake uses MoSim InteractionRoller contacts and bounded transfer traction. A dedicated two-floor indexer follows the original CAD: five lower roller axes at native heights 0.137-0.193 m and four upper axes at 0.283-0.339 m. The upper floor carries fuel to a physical drop into the lower entrance. The former 32.2-degree rising feed ramp is removed. Passive roller cores and a shallow landing support fuel even when stopped. The 12.5 mm-radius visual/contact rollers use 6.5 mm-radius hard cores to represent fuel compression at the roughly 145 mm inter-deck gap while leaving the official 150 mm fuel unchanged.

The two small triangular side-indexer assemblies are passive in this model. Their convex support envelopes come from the original 26-413-001 CAD faces, without powered traction or driven animation. Gravity, contact with neighboring fuel and hopper pumping move shoulder fuel toward the lower mouth. They replace the broad inferred shoulder wedges.

The shooter has 0.313 m of clear width, enough for two official 150 mm balls side by side. A lower-entry handoff must be observed before the accelerator or flywheels can grip a ball, including fuel arriving from the upper floor. The accelerator follows a curved internal path from the bottom opening to the existing release channel. One or two balls pass according to physical arrival; there is no pairing queue. Lower-entry and flywheel-readiness gates retain staged fuel while aiming or spooling. Each gate waits for its own volume to clear before closing, so an interlock cannot close through entering fuel. During a firing-state re-aim, the simulator feed interlock stops the accelerator as well as the indexer; its finite contact forces brake staged fuel instead of continuing to push a stream through the occupied gate. Reverse recovery leaves that brake released.

All transport uses bounded local forces and equal chassis reaction. Indexer traction is shared across both floors with a 320 N total budget. Forward 78 motor RPS maps to an explicitly calibrated 1.2 m/s roller surface speed; reverse is -20 motor RPS. The accelerator adapter maps its 2200 RPM reference proportionally to 2.5 m/s, with separate 60000 reference/contact RPM per second ramps and a 320 N budget, and the wide flywheel contact shares a 480 N budget. These are simulator contact calibrations, not measured hardware ratios. Every ball touching a shooter stage in the same physics step sees the same wheel speed before combined arrival load is applied. Fuel remains independent, with no parenting, repositioning, freezing, velocity assignment or corrections in flight.

The storage floor ends at the stationary roller bed instead of covering its lower axes. Its forward overlap follows the physical hopper front panel while the sliding walls push fuel toward the indexer. The intake roller and all nine indexer roller meshes rotate around their measured CAD axes. `Tools/Blender/prepare_indexer.py` preserves existing game roller faces and reads the reference CAD without saving it; `Reports/indexer-model.json` records the geometry.

The intake side-guide proxies end at native X = 0.33 m, at the first lower roller. Their forward pickup extent is preserved. The former rear caps extended into the triangular shoulder area and intercepted corner fuel before it could touch the CAD support; the triangles now own that passive handoff.

The indexer's bundle data uses `Transform[]` and `Vector3[]` for the nine visual references, axes and dimensions. Its public roller definitions are reconstructed from those Unity-owned types. Installed-player inspection found that the original nested managed `Roller[]` deserialized as an empty array, although it worked in the editor; the physical cores remained but native traction and roller animation had no definitions. Native inspection now verifies all nine measured axes and mesh references after loading.

The intake uses an explicit one-way contact approximation. Incoming fuel follows a local curved contact path from the ground pickup into the tray. During entry, only this robot's storage-floor, inlet-keeper and front-panel contact pairs can open. The floors become solid as soon as the sphere clears them; the front gate closes after the sphere clears the inlet plane and settles vertically at the handoff. Reverse opens the front gate while keeping the floors under stored fuel solid. Pairs are also restored when fuel exits the area or the keeper is disabled. No global game-piece rules change. Fuel is never parented, frozen, repositioned, or assigned a storage velocity.

Floor clearance is latched until the whole sphere exits the front or side of the hopper. A bounce or compression from the load cannot reopen the floor. Powered intake contact follows a narrow 60 mm roller/throat path, rising over the upper kicker before handing fuel into storage. A continuous backing surface supports this belt path, preventing incoming ground fuel from rolling underneath the storage floor when the hopper fills. Normal force is bounded to 80 N and the shared mechanism budget to 600 N, with a 3.6 m/s surface-speed ceiling. Retention classification does not cut power before this handoff finishes. The stock fuel mass, diameter and collision material remain unchanged.

Turbo excludes fuel layers from the stock drivetrain's suspension and powered tread queries. Its solid tire meshes, module housings, bellypan, bumpers and hopper still collide with fuel. The wheels continue to sample the field normally, while rolling balls remain passive obstacles.

Tread friction is calibrated to 1.4, with the existing 0.32 bite factor. The earlier 1.3 configuration intermittently missed the short loaded-reversal speed criterion and the 3-degree wheel-path drift bound. The lower 95 N intake-traction comparison improved drift but did not consistently satisfy loaded speed; the collection budget remains 100 N. The team's drive speed, gearing, motor gains and current limits are unchanged. These are simulator contact estimates, not measured tire/carpet coefficients.

The fixed-frame net uses 320 particles representing a 240 g textile. Its contact radius is 1.15 mm, matching the source model's estimated 2.3 mm cord diameter. The previous 12 mm radius modeled a 24 mm cord and consumed packing clearance. Rest-length scale 1.0, stretch compliance 3e-7 and shear compliance 1.5e-7 calibrate a tensioned textile; bend compliance remains .002 so it can fold locally. Fuel contacts run on every cloth substep, rather than every other substep, to reduce constraint tunneling during crossbar compression. These are simulator calibration. Thin frame-edge contacts complete the fixed lacing boundaries that the native cloth solver skips when both edge nodes are pinned. The original triangular side-indexer envelopes support the shoulders without covering the lower mouth.

`Turbo4744NetEnvironment` joins the native cloth job and resolves free-node contacts against static field boxes, including the 564 mm-high trench crossbar. Contact velocity includes chassis motion; the external contact impulse reaches the chassis. It does not move fuel or alter the field. The stock solver continues handling fuel spheres; field meshes are outside this adapter's scope.

`Turbo4744FuelCompliance` limits the solver's normal impulse for fuel-to-fuel contacts inside the hopper, approximating compressible foam with 750 N/m stiffness and 8 N·s/m closing damping. At 20 mm penetration the original hard contact takes over; solver-step transients can exceed that transition depth. This is a calibrated contact law, rather than a measured material fit; the [FIRST/AndyMark testing report](https://www.firstinspires.org/hubfs/blog/frc/2026-scoring-element-whitepaper-am.pdf) establishes that FUEL has a variable compression response. Original sphere geometry, mass and field/mechanism contacts remain unchanged. Contacts return to their original flags after leaving the hopper or disabling the component. A published immutable snapshot keeps the physics-thread callback away from Unity object access.

The earlier 1.05-rest-length comparison used the old thick cord and rigid fuel contacts; it failed capacity and remains preserved. A later thin-cord/compliant-fuel candidate with the same 5% ease lost stored fuel over the native crossbar. Swept-node contacts also failed by catching and stretching the roof. Both comparisons remain recorded; the current calibration uses rest scale 1.0 and analytic box contacts. Tightening the net alone is insufficient. The real hopper stays extended when fuel physically prevents retraction. Native acceptance separately checks full-load blocked-stow retention, shooting and empty-stow recovery, plus partially loaded stowed trench/bump transit. Every transit cohort member must remain, with zero escapes and less than .25 s stopped under the bar. Full-hopper cycles use ordinary input actions and the original 504 field fuel bodies. See [current native checks](NativeTesting.md).

Flow monitoring accumulates inward travel in 6 mm steps and downward or upward travel in 25 mm steps, recognizing both the upper-floor drop and internal accelerator lift. Slow but continuing tray motion counts as progress; a stationary obstruction still triggers the bounded reverse recovery. Movement back toward an already observed extreme does not keep resetting the stall timer.

The backing surface ends at the kicker release, leaving the storage tray clear. The last two intake path segments hand incoming fuel down onto the lower roller bed, avoiding a fast approach rebounding from the upper floor. The backing engages after full initial deployment, stays available during pumping and clears before full stow. Collection uses a 100 N shared tangential motor budget at 3.6 m/s, with passive support preserved under the 600 N overall contact cap. Reducing the overall cap alone removed support as well as traction and degraded dense arrivals. An active shot retains the existing 140 N overall / 1.2 m/s transfer profile to return stored fuel from the entry ramp to the indexer. Retained fuel resting on the floor remains eligible for feeding, rather than requiring an air gap under the sphere. Collection traction stops after full inlet clearance and latched storage support. Feeding can re-engage this entry contact with the lower-speed transfer profile.

This is a collision proxy for intake retention. The intake command stays latched at the team's 90 rotor RPS during collection. Disabled mode stops all rollers while retaining stored fuel through the solid contacts.

Drive and shooter tables come from the team's 2026 code. Motor-to-wheel conversion, contact geometry, friction, launch channel, and mass distribution are simulation calibration. The roof uses MoSim's deforming cloth solver with a sliding front frame and stationary rear lacing, rather than mesh scaling or a hard roof. [The code mapping](RobotCodePort.md) records the pinned source, active state transitions, and hardware limitations.

The hopper roof material is derived from MoSim's `Assets/Materials/Net/Net.mat` and uses its supplied `ActualTileableNet.png`. The texture already contains multiple openings, so the roof tiles it twice in each direction. The earlier 18 by 16 tiling made the holes too small to remain visible at normal camera distances. Both sides render with alpha-clipped openings, depth writes and shadows; the texture supplies the strand color without an additional dark tint.

Incoming fuel receives bounded transfer traction while touching the modeled roller path. The contact follows the intake rail and applies its reaction to that moving body. Its edge falloff spans the tray width, keeping support beneath a ball against either side guide. Retraction preserves the existing motor command and moves the contact with the intake; disable stops it. Floor support latches as soon as a ball clears the tray surface, independently of the front wall's stricter full-sphere clearance. Fuel remains an independent rigidbody.

The helper intake and accelerator roller cores exclude fuel, and their native traction is disabled. Their custom contacts own fuel forces without duplicate wheel-core impulses. The indexer's nine passive capsule cores support fuel; its dedicated traction acts only near the measured roller surfaces and limits speed to 1.2 m/s at 78 motor RPS. Fuel on either floor remains supported while the indexer stops, reverses or recovers a jam. The pickup does not brake incoming fuel that is already moving inward faster than the belt.

The approach regression offers nine ground balls at 0.5, 2 and 4 m/s. The faster cases include balls at both intake edges and multiple robot headings. It measures retained pickups, travel and chassis tilt through braking, disable and loaded stow. Use `-Task Approach -Authoring` during contact tuning or `-Task Approach` to check the built bundle; `-Baseline` writes a separate before report.

During a firing-state re-aim, the accelerator contact receives a zero-speed brake target directly. The motor reference keeps its existing ramp, while the contact keeps its 60000 RPM/s ramp and finite 320 N force budget. Waiting for that motor reference delayed braking long enough for staged fuel to enter the flywheels during a direction reversal. The preserved moving-shot failure and separate brake comparison record the diagnosis.

## Verification

The integration check uses the real preload spawn handler and normal keyboard action bindings. It exercises retained preloads while driving, deployment, pickup, the same ball feeding through the shooter, disabled shutdown, and stowing.

The travel regression loads the Windows asset bundle, rather than the authoring prefab. It samples travel every physics step at 0°, 90°, 180°, and -90° headings. Each case includes a held intake command, driving/turning, disable/enable, and stow. An equal-and-opposite 40 N·s actuator impulse deliberately loads the intake end stop. This fault-injection test checks that physical bounds remain effective even when a controller alone could not stop the mechanism immediately.

The retention regression starts empty and collects a group of nine official field fuel objects. It follows each captured ball through a held intake command, release, forward/reverse driving, 90°/s turns, curved motion, disable, and loaded stow at two initial headings. It requires at least seven pickups, retention of every collected ball, no escapes, stable robot motion, and successful stow. Its report records the tested asset bundle hash. The earlier 0.2.0 reproduction captured nine balls in its first case and subsequently lost all nine. The revised test uses clear neutral-zone carpet with unrelated fuel disabled, keeping field structures and loose background fuel out of the collection route.

The systems benchmark measures driving with an empty hopper and a seeded 20-piece load, checking how many remain before and after maneuvering. It also starts eight real preloads at each shooting distance, requests a three-cycle hard pump, and counts actual scoring events in the hub. Drive commands are applied at every physics step. Cloth scale, deployment span, finite particles, and rail motion are recorded alongside scoring and speed measurements. Reports fingerprint the packaged bundle and runtime DLL.

Total intake force is shared across a dense pile. Pumping slides the hopper walls and front roof frame toward the stationary indexer. The storage tray overlaps the fixed inlet throughout travel. The partial-load benchmark measures the actual front-panel movement as well as the intake encoder, and requires the seeded balls to score or deliver. F8 toggles a local status panel and per-second diagnostic log for native-game verification.

Side keeper surfaces retain shoulder fuel beside the shooter. The passive triangular supports and wide lower guides leave the upper-floor drop open.

Regression collection/retention counts include the lower roller bed and the narrow accelerator staging volume behind the hopper. Fuel there remains inside the robot even when the old rising-ramp hopper box would exclude it. Existing offered/retained counts, escape checks, drive and scoring thresholds remain unchanged.

Loaded-stow retention derives the available volume from the actual moving front panel. A fixed front cutoff cannot describe the sliding bin. Retention still requires every collected ball to remain inside with zero escapes. The earlier fixed-bin full-load stow results do not certify this revised mechanism; packed-load stow needs a fresh native run.

Lower-mouth admission tests the physical ball surface against the configured mouth top at local Y 0.275 m. A ball can begin contacting that opening while its centre remains above it. The previous centre-only test missed partial entries under mixed loads. The lane, mouth plane, stock fuel radius and force budgets remain unchanged; upper-floor fuel still has to descend before the accelerator or shooter contact may engage.
